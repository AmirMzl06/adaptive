# Experiment 9 — Investigating the Failure of Spatial d′-Adversarial Training in Behavior-Supervised CEBRA

**Research Progress Report — E1, E2, and E2b**

## 1. Motivation

Our previous experiments established that noise-aware adversarial training can substantially improve the robustness of CEBRA representations against noise-shaped perturbations. However, Experiment 8 revealed an important limitation: while difference-residual adversarial training (DIFF8) remained robust in behavior-supervised CEBRA, the whole-covariance spatial method (SP8) became highly vulnerable in several recording sessions.

One possible explanation was **robust overfitting**: because the number of training iterations was fixed at 3,000, shorter recordings were exposed to substantially more training epochs. This could potentially cause the model to overfit adversarial perturbations or learn an unstable representation.

Experiment 9 was designed to investigate this explanation before proceeding with the proposed automatic training-budget method, AUTO_SIG.

We conducted three controlled experiments, E1, E2, and E2b, comprising **42 new training runs**.

## 2. Experimental Design

All experiments used behavior-supervised CEBRA, with the encoder trained using both neural spike counts and behavioral labels:

`encoder.fit(train_data, train_label)`

We compared two training methods:

- **PGD6:** Conventional adversarial training with a fixed raw-L2 perturbation budget of ε = 6.
- **SP8:** Noise-aware adversarial training with d′ = 8, using the whole-residual Ledoit–Wolf spatial covariance.

Every configuration was evaluated using the same reference (noise-shaped) and isotropic robustness metrics as in Experiment 8.

Three random seeds (42, 43, and 44) were used for each configuration.

| Experiment | Configuration | Sessions | Runs |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| E1 | Reduce training to approximately 340 epochs in five previously brittle sessions | 5 | 30 |
| E2 | Reduce C-CO12 training data to 9,174 bins while keeping 3,000 iterations | 1 | 6 |
| E2b | Keep the full C-CO12 recording and increase training to 6,000 iterations | 1 | 6 |
| **Total** | | | **42** |

All **42/42 runs completed successfully**, with no reported failures.

## 3. Experiment E1 — Does Reducing the Number of Epochs Restore Robustness?

### 3.1 Setup

We selected five sessions in which SP8 had previously exhibited severe robustness degradation:

C-RT6, M-RT3, T-RT1, T-RT4, and J-CO0.

Instead of using 3,000 iterations regardless of recording length, we set training to approximately 340 effective epochs.

This resulted in 634–1,902 iterations, depending on the number of training bins in each session.

Both SP8 and PGD6 were retrained with the same reduced epoch budget for three seeds.

### 3.2 Results

**Reducing the number of epochs did not restore SP8 robustness.**

In all 15 SP8 runs, the reference half-damage threshold remained at the smallest tested perturbation budget, d′ = 0.5.

A threshold reported as 0.5 is lower-grid clipped: the true half-damage threshold may be below 0.5.

By comparison, PGD6 reached this floor in only 3 of the 15 runs.

| Session | Mean reference ε½, SP8 | Mean reference ε½, PGD6 | SP8 improvement? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| C-RT6 | ≤0.500 | 0.906 | No |
| J-CO0 | ≤0.500 | 0.858 | No |
| M-RT3 | ≤0.500 | 1.120 | No |
| T-RT1 | ≤0.500 | ≤0.500 | Both clipped |
| T-RT4 | ≤0.500 | 0.690 | No |

Across all 15 paired runs:

| Metric | SP8 relative to PGD6 |
|---|---:|
| Mean difference in clean validation R² | −0.0233 |
| Mean difference in reference ε½ | −0.3147 |
| Mean difference in isotropic ε½ | −1.1227 |
| Strict reference robustness wins | **0/15** |
| SP8 runs clipped at reference ε½ = 0.5 | **15/15** |

These outcomes closely reproduce the failure pattern observed in the original Experiment 8, despite the shorter training duration.

Importantly, the SP8 training loss gains ranged from approximately 0.63 to 1.29. Thus, the brittleness cannot simply be attributed to the extreme loss-gain saturation previously observed in the most aggressive training configurations.

### 3.3 Interpretation

The results provide evidence **against the hypothesis that excessive training epochs alone explain SP8's failure**.

Even when the number of epochs was substantially reduced, SP8 remained vulnerable in every tested seed of the five brittle sessions.

The fact that PGD6 did not exhibit the same degree of failure under the matched training schedule further suggests that the problem is associated with the interaction between the noise-shaped training perturbations, the training objective, and session-specific signal structure.

However, these results do not completely exclude other forms of robust overfitting or training instability. They specifically show that simply reducing the number of training epochs is insufficient to resolve the observed brittleness.

## 4. Experiment E2 — Does Reducing the Amount of Training Data Cause the Failure?

### 4.1 Setup

We selected C-CO12, a recording where SP8 had previously remained robust.

The training set was reduced from 18,384 to 9,174 bins, while retaining 3,000 optimization iterations.

This increased the effective number of training epochs to approximately 670.

Both SP8 and PGD6 were trained using three random seeds.

### 4.2 Results

SP8 remained robust despite the reduction in training data.

| Seed | SP8 clean R² | PGD6 clean R² | SP8 reference ε½ | PGD6 reference ε½ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 42 | 0.6904 | 0.6850 | 2.3818 | 2.2124 |
| 43 | 0.6792 | 0.6649 | 2.5194 | 2.2723 |
| 44 | 0.6820 | 0.6779 | 2.2707 | 2.2050 |

Summary:

- SP8 reference robustness wins: **3/3**
- Mean clean R² difference: **+0.0079**
- Mean reference half-damage threshold difference: **+0.1608**
- Mean isotropic half-damage threshold difference: **−2.0631**
- No SP8 run was clipped at the smallest reference budget.

### 4.3 Interpretation

Reducing the amount of training data did not reproduce the SP8 failure in this otherwise stable session.

SP8 retained a consistent advantage against the reference threat model while maintaining slightly higher clean decoding performance than PGD6.

The isotropic robustness trade-off remained present.

This suggests that reducing data availability alone is not sufficient to explain the catastrophic brittleness observed in the five difficult sessions. However, this intervention was performed on one previously stable session, so it cannot establish that data quantity is unimportant in every recording.

## 5. Experiment E2b — Does Increasing the Number of Iterations Cause the Failure?

### 5.1 Setup

As a complementary control, we kept the complete C-CO12 recording (18,384 bins) but increased training to 6,000 iterations.

This produced approximately 668 effective epochs, closely matching E2's epoch count while using twice as much training data.

### 5.2 Results

SP8 remained robust after the longer training schedule.

| Seed | SP8 clean R² | PGD6 clean R² | SP8 reference ε½ | PGD6 reference ε½ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 42 | 0.6913 | 0.6736 | 2.6297 | 2.4229 |
| 43 | 0.6897 | 0.6849 | 3.3755 | 2.4510 |
| 44 | 0.6868 | 0.6835 | 2.7591 | 2.4184 |

Summary:

- SP8 reference robustness wins: **3/3**
- Mean clean R² difference: **+0.0086**
- Mean reference half-damage threshold difference: **+0.4907**
- Mean isotropic half-damage threshold difference: **−2.1932**
- No SP8 run was clipped at the smallest reference budget.

The reference robustness advantage of SP8 was larger in E2b than in E2, although this observation comes from only three seeds of one session.

### 5.3 Interpretation

Increasing the total number of optimization iterations to 6,000 did not cause SP8 to collapse in C-CO12.

Taken together, E2 and E2b indicate that neither reducing the available training data nor substantially increasing the number of training iterations is sufficient, by itself, to reproduce the failure in a session where SP8 is normally stable.

This supports the interpretation that **session-specific signal and noise structure is a major factor in SP8 robustness**.

## 6. Comparison Across E1, E2, and E2b

| Result | E1: Five brittle sessions | E2: C-CO12, reduced data | E2b: C-CO12, longer training |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| New training runs | 30 | 6 | 6 |
| SP8 reference wins over PGD6 | 0/15 | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| Mean Δ clean R² | −0.0233 | +0.0079 | +0.0086 |
| Mean Δ reference ε½ | −0.3147 | +0.1608 | +0.4907 |
| SP8 runs with clipped reference ε½ | 15/15 | 0/3 | 0/3 |

The contrast between the brittle and stable sessions is striking.

The five difficult sessions remained vulnerable despite shorter training, whereas the stable C-CO12 session preserved SP8's reference robustness advantage under both reduced-data and extended-training conditions.

Together, these results weaken an explanation based solely on training duration or the effective number of epochs.

They are more consistent with the hypothesis that **whole-residual covariance can permit training perturbations that interfere with behaviorally relevant neural structure**, depending on the recording.

This interpretation is also consistent with the broader findings from Experiment 8: difference-residual training (DIFF8) remained substantially more robust across behavior-supervised sessions than whole-residual SP8.

Nevertheless, E1/E2/E2b do not directly prove the signal-contamination mechanism. That mechanism remains a hypothesis requiring further validation.

## 7. Implications for Automatic Epsilon Selection

These results strengthen the motivation for a training-budget rule that accounts for the relationship between perturbation magnitude and natural signal separation, rather than using detectability alone.

Our proposed AUTO_SIG rule is:

\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{train}}
=
\min\left(
(2D)^{1/4},
\;0.24\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}(M)
\right)
\]

where \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}(M)\) estimates the separation between natural signal states under the corresponding training metric.

The conceptual motivation is that an adversarial perturbation should not become so large, relative to natural signal changes, that the encoder is encouraged to discard behaviorally meaningful information.

The earlier retrospective analysis showed that the normalized ratio

\[
\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{train}}}
{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}(M)}
\]

was associated with decoder brittleness in supervised CEBRA.

The new E1/E2/E2b results make this direction more relevant because reducing training duration did not solve the SP8 failure.

However, **AUTO_SIG has not yet been trained or prospectively validated**. The retrospective association and the epoch-control experiments do not establish that the proposed automatic budget will improve decoder robustness.

The coefficient 0.24 is also informed by our existing Perich analyses; external validation will be necessary before claiming general dataset-independent calibration.

## 8. Conclusions and Recommended Next Step

The 42 completed runs support three conclusions:

**First**, SP8's catastrophic reference-geometry brittleness in behavior-supervised CEBRA persists after substantially reducing the number of training epochs.

**Second**, an otherwise robust session remains robust when the amount of training data is reduced or the number of optimization iterations is doubled.

**Third**, the results favor further investigation of session-dependent perturbation geometry and signal preservation over a simple training-duration explanation.

I therefore suggest proceeding with the planned AUTO_SIG experiments as a separate, controlled extension, rather than modifying the established DIFF8 and POOL baselines.

The prepared Experiment 9 runner includes the following proposed follow-up suites:

| Follow-up suite | Mode | Configurations | Planned runs |
|---|---|---|---:|
| AUTO_SIG supervised | Behavior-supervised | SPDIFF_AUTO, SP_AUTO, SPREG_AUTO, SP_POOL_AUTO | 108 |
| AUTO_SIG time-only | Time-only | SPDIFF_AUTO, SP_AUTO, SP_POOL_AUTO | 81 |
| **Total** | | | **189** |

The main evaluation should compare these automatic-budget methods against the already completed fixed-budget models on matched sessions and seeds, with **fixed raw-L2 PGD6 as the primary baseline**.

The key success criterion should be improved reference-geometry robustness while preserving clean decoding performance, especially in sessions where the original whole-covariance spatial method was brittle.

We should also track the frequency of clipped half-damage thresholds and performance under the isotropic threat model, to ensure that improvements do not simply move the failure to another attack geometry.

**Overall conclusion:** Experiment 9 provides evidence that the supervised SP8 failure is not resolved by controlling the number of epochs alone. It motivates, but does not yet validate, a signal-aware automatic training-budget mechanism. The next step is to determine whether AUTO_SIG can prevent this brittleness while retaining the reference-geometry robustness gains observed with fixed-budget DIFF8 and POOL.
