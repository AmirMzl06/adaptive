There are several mathematical gaps in the proposed derivation that should be addressed before presenting \(x^*=1/(2\sqrt{2})\approx0.354\) as a theoretically justified optimal or safe adversarial budget.

**1. The derivation of 0.354 is algebraically correct, but its optimality is not established.**

For two independent, identically distributed samples in the whitened space, the identity

\[
\mathbb{E}\|s_a-s_b\|^2=2\sigma^2
\]

is correct, provided that \(\sigma^2=\mathbb{E}\|s-\bar{s}\|^2\). Consequently, defining \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{task}}\) as the root-mean-square pairwise distance gives \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{task}}=\sqrt{2}\sigma\).

However, the additional choice \(\epsilon^*=\sigma/2\) does not follow from this identity. Why should half the RMS distance from the distribution mean be the largest safe perturbation? A formal optimization objective, risk constraint, or additional geometric assumption is required to justify this choice.

**2. RMS separation does not guarantee non-overlapping adversarial regions.**

The midpoint argument establishes that two perturbation balls intersect when their radii reach half the actual distance between their centers. However, the proposed derivation substitutes an expected RMS distance for that pairwise distance.

These quantities are not interchangeable. Even if the RMS separation is large, some positive–negative pairs may be arbitrarily close.

A genuine sufficient condition for non-overlap would be

\[
\epsilon < \frac12\min_{(a,b)\in\mathcal P_-}\|s_a-s_b\|,
\]

where \(\mathcal P_-\) denotes the relevant negative pairs.

Therefore, \(\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{task}}/(2\sqrt2)\) does not generally guarantee the absence of overlap.

**3. Overlap does not necessarily imply representation collapse.**

Even if adversarial regions corresponding to different contrastive targets overlap, this establishes a conflict in simultaneously satisfying certain robustness constraints. It does not prove that the entire CEBRA embedding collapses.

Such a conclusion would require explicit assumptions about the contrastive objective, positive/negative sampling, encoder capacity, and the relationship between adversarial overlap and the collapse criterion.

Similarly, the proposed decoder \(R^2\) lower bound does not follow from the stated geometric lemma without additional assumptions about the data-generating process and decoder.

**4. The claimed threshold is not a distribution-free guarantee.**

The empirical absence of failures below \(x^*\) in Exp14–15 is encouraging, but it cannot establish that the failure probability is zero. The fitted logistic collapse-risk model is also empirical evidence, not a proof of the theoretical threshold.

The distinction between a deterministic geometric guarantee, a probabilistic risk bound, and an empirically observed low-risk region needs to be made explicit.

**5. The Time-Only coefficient is not fully derived without empirical calibration.**

The proposed conversion using

\[
\rho=\frac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{beh}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{sig}}}
\]

gives \(0.354\times0.83\approx0.293\) at the reported median ratio, rather than the existing coefficient 0.24.

Recovering 0.24 requires choosing approximately \(\rho=0.68\), corresponding to an empirical lower percentile. Unless that percentile is independently justified by a specified risk criterion, the resulting coefficient remains data-calibrated rather than purely theoretical.

**6. A safe budget is not necessarily the robustness-optimal budget.**

Even if a non-overlap threshold could be rigorously established, it would only provide a geometric safety condition. It would not demonstrate that the corresponding perturbation budget maximizes adversarial robustness or downstream representation quality.

The Exp15 results already illustrate this distinction: robustness can decline before any observed collapse, particularly in Time-Only training.

**Suggested theoretical direction**

A more defensible approach would be to formulate a probabilistic separation bound using the distribution of positive–negative distances in the noise-whitened space.

For example, if \(D_-\) denotes the distance between a sampled negative pair, then choosing

\[
\epsilon\leq\frac12 Q_\alpha(D_-)
\]

would bound the probability of pairwise adversarial-region overlap by \(\alpha\), under the specified sampling distribution.

This would yield a mathematically interpretable geometric risk bound, although connecting that bound to actual CEBRA collapse would still require separate theoretical assumptions or empirical validation.

Overall, \(1/(2\sqrt2)\) is an interesting theoretically motivated candidate, and its proximity to 0.363 is worth investigating. However, the current derivation does not establish that it is a universally safe threshold or an optimal adversarial budget. The main missing step is a rigorous connection between RMS signal geometry, pairwise adversarial overlap, representation collapse, and robustness optimization.
