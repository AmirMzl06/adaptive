# Research Proposal: Self-Supervised Neural Subpopulation Information Ranking

## 1. Research Motivation

The goal is to learn meaningful latent representations of neural population activity **without behavioral labels during pretraining, spike reconstruction, or neural activity forecasting**.

Our central hypothesis is that imposing an information-based ordering across nested neural subpopulations can encourage an encoder to capture meaningful inter-neuronal dependencies and population dynamics.

Consider a neural recording containing **96 simultaneously recorded neurons**, represented as:

\[
X \in \mathbb{R}^{96 \times T}
\]

where \(T\) denotes the temporal window length.

During self-supervised pretraining, we divide the recorded neurons into two non-overlapping populations:

- **Population A:** 80 neurons used to construct input representations.
- **Population B:** 16 neurons serving as an independent reference population.

From Population A, we construct five nested subsets:

\[
A_{10} \subset A_{50} \subset A_{60} \subset A_{70} \subset A_{80}
\]

Since each larger population contains all neurons from the smaller population, the following information-theoretic inequality holds for the raw neural variables:

\[
I(X_{A_{10}};X_B)
\leq I(X_{A_{50}};X_B)
\leq I(X_{A_{60}};X_B)
\leq I(X_{A_{70}};X_B)
\leq I(X_{A_{80}};X_B)
\]

This follows from the chain rule of mutual information and the non-negativity of conditional mutual information.

However, this ordering is **not automatically guaranteed for learned embeddings or finite-sample estimates of mutual information**.

We therefore propose investigating whether incorporating an analogous ordinal constraint into representation learning improves the quality of neural latent spaces.

## 2. Proposed Encoder Architecture

A key architectural challenge is handling neural subsets containing different numbers of neurons without zero-padding missing channels.

We propose a shared encoder capable of accepting variable-sized neural populations while producing fixed-dimensional representations.

### Architecture

**Input**

\[
X_{A_k} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times T}
\]

where \(k \in \{10,50,60,70,80\}\).

↓

**Shared Temporal Convolutional Network**

- Applies the same temporal convolutional filters to each neuron.
- Extracts temporal features from individual neuronal activity.
- Supports variable numbers of neurons.
- Incorporates learnable neuron identity embeddings to preserve neuronal identity within a recording session.

↓

**Neuron-Level Self-Attention**

- Models interactions and dependencies among recorded neurons.
- Allows the network to capture information beyond individual firing patterns.
- Avoids imposing an artificial spatial neighborhood on the ordering of neuronal channels.

↓

**Attention Pooling**

- Aggregates variable numbers of neuronal tokens into a fixed-dimensional population representation.

↓

**Projection Head**

\[
z_k = f_{\theta}(X_{A_k}) \in \mathbb{R}^{64}
\]

Thus:

\[
A_{10} \rightarrow z_{10}, \quad
A_{50} \rightarrow z_{50}, \quad
A_{60} \rightarrow z_{60}
\]

\[
A_{70} \rightarrow z_{70}, \quad
A_{80} \rightarrow z_{80}
\]

All subsets are processed using **the same encoder parameters**.

A separate reference encoder, \(g_\phi\), maps Population B into the same embedding space.

Importantly, the architecture contains **no reconstruction decoder**.

## 3. Self-Supervised Learning Objective

We use a contrastive matching objective to encourage embeddings derived from Population A to retain information shared with Population B.

For each neural population size \(k\), we compute:

\[
z_k=f_\theta(X_{A_k})
\]

\[
u=g_\phi(X_B)
\]

Positive pairs correspond to the same temporal window, while negative pairs correspond to appropriately separated temporal windows.

The contrastive objective for each population size is:

\[
\mathcal{L}_k^{\mathrm{NCE}}
=
-\log
\frac{\exp(\operatorname{sim}(z_k,u^+)/\tau)}
{\sum_j \exp(\operatorname{sim}(z_k,u_j)/\tau)}
\]

The baseline self-supervised objective is:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SSL}}
=
\frac{1}{5}
\sum_{k \in \{10,50,60,70,80\}}
\mathcal{L}_k^{\mathrm{NCE}}
\]

### Information-Based Ranking Constraint

Our hypothesis is that, as additional neurons become available, the representation should become more informative about the independent reference population.

We therefore encourage the following ordering of expected contrastive losses:

\[
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{80}]
\leq
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{70}]
\leq
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{60}]
\leq
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{50}]
\leq
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{10}]
\]

An ordinal regularizer can be defined as:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rank}}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{4}
\max\left(0,\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k_{i+1}}-
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k_i}\right)
\]

where:

\[
(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4,k_5)=(10,50,60,70,80)
\]

and the bars denote losses averaged over a batch or a more stable collection of samples.

The complete objective becomes:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SSL}}
+
\lambda \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rank}}
}
\]

We do not explicitly estimate high-dimensional mutual information. Instead, InfoNCE provides a practical contrastive surrogate.

An important limitation is that the ranking constraint could also be satisfied by degrading representations from smaller subsets. This makes comparison against standard contrastive learning essential.

## 4. Proposed Experimental Configurations

We propose five experimental conditions using the same underlying encoder architecture.

### Experiment 1: Fixed Target Population With Ranking

**Configuration**

- A fixed set of 80 neurons forms Population A.
- A fixed set of 16 neurons forms Population B.
- Five nested subsets are sampled from Population A.
- Shared Temporal CNN, Neuron Self-Attention, and Attention Pooling.
- Objective: InfoNCE + Ordinal Ranking.

**Research question**

Can the network learn meaningful information ordering when the reference population remains fixed throughout training?

**Potential limitation**

The selected target neurons may contain limited information about the remaining population, resulting in a weak contrastive learning signal.

### Experiment 2: Dynamic Target Population With Ranking

**Configuration**

- At every training batch, randomly select 16 neurons to form Population B.
- The remaining 80 neurons form Population A.
- Construct five nested subsets containing 10, 50, 60, 70, and 80 neurons.
- Use the same shared encoder architecture.
- Objective: InfoNCE + Ordinal Ranking.

**Research question**

Does dynamically changing the reference population encourage the model to learn broader inter-neuronal relationships rather than dependencies involving a particular fixed subset?

**Hypothesis**

Random population partitioning may reduce sensitivity to the quality of any individual reference population and expose the encoder to a wider range of population interactions.

This is our primary proposed configuration.

### Experiment 3: Dynamic Target Population Without Ranking

**Configuration**

Identical to Experiment 2, except the ordinal ranking loss is removed.

**Objective**

\[
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SSL}}
\]

**Research question**

Does the information-ordering constraint provide a measurable benefit beyond standard cross-population contrastive representation learning?

**Importance**

This is the most important ablation for isolating the contribution of the proposed ranking objective.

Any observed improvement in Experiment 2 must be evaluated against this baseline under matched training conditions.

### Experiment 4: Dynamic Target Population With Disrupted Neural Coordination

**Configuration**

Use the architecture and objective from Experiment 2, but introduce controlled perturbations that disrupt inter-neuronal coordination during pretraining.

For example, constrained spike-raster swaps can preserve:

- The total spike count of each neuron.
- The total population spike count at each time bin.

while modifying certain temporal relationships between neuronal channels.

**Objective**

\[
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SSL}}
+
\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rank}}
\]

**Research question**

Does the learned representation depend on meaningful neural population coordination, or can the objective be satisfied using simple statistical properties such as firing rates?

**Expected interpretation**

If controlled disruption of population coordination reduces downstream decoding performance, this would support the hypothesis that inter-neuronal dependencies contribute to representation quality.

However, the perturbation may also affect other temporal statistics, which must be monitored.

### Experiment 5: Dynamic Target Population With Shuffled Ranking

**Configuration**

Identical to Experiment 2, except that the ordinal targets associated with population sizes are randomly permuted.

The underlying contrastive task, input subsets, encoder architecture, and training budget remain unchanged.

**Objective**

\[
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SSL}}
+
\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{shuffled\ rank}}
\]

**Research question**

Is the correct information ordering important, or are any observed improvements primarily due to the addition of an auxiliary regularization term?

**Importance**

This experiment serves as a negative control for the ranking mechanism.

## 5. Experimental Comparison

| Experiment | Target Population B | Ranking Objective | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Fixed B + Ranking | Fixed | Correct ordering | Fixed-target baseline |
| 2. Dynamic B + Ranking | Randomized per batch | Correct ordering | Main proposed method |
| 3. Dynamic B, No Ranking | Randomized per batch | None | Isolate the benefit of ranking |
| 4. Dynamic B + Coordination Disruption | Randomized per batch | Correct ordering | Test sensitivity to neural coordination |
| 5. Dynamic B + Shuffled Ranking | Randomized per batch | Randomized ordering | Negative control for ordinal supervision |

All experimental conditions should use matched encoder capacity, initialization protocols, training iterations, data splits, and downstream evaluation procedures.

## 6. Downstream Evaluation

The method will be evaluated on neural population recordings, such as motor-cortex datasets from Perich et al.

### Self-Supervised Pretraining

Only neural activity is used.

- No behavioral labels.
- No spike reconstruction.
- No forecasting of neural activity.
- No supervised behavioral decoding objective.

### Representation Extraction

After pretraining, the encoder is used to extract latent representations of neural activity.

Since the proposed architecture supports variable numbers of neurons, we can investigate using all 96 available neurons at inference.

However, the model is trained primarily with subsets of at most 80 input neurons. Therefore, generalization to 96 neurons must be explicitly evaluated rather than assumed.

### Behavioral Decoding

A fixed downstream decoder is trained using the frozen neural representations and behavioral labels.

We propose:

- Linear or Ridge Regression Probe.
- A fixed two-layer MLP Decoder.

### Evaluation Metrics

- Behavioral decoding \(R^2\).
- Mean Squared Error.
- Pearson Correlation.
- Effective Rank and covariance structure of learned representations.
- Held-out ordinal ranking accuracy.
- Sensitivity to neural coordination disruption.
- Performance across multiple random seeds.

### Baselines

The proposed method should be compared with:

- Randomly initialized encoders.
- Standard cross-population InfoNCE.
- CEBRA-Time.
- The proposed method without ranking.
- The proposed method with shuffled ranking targets.

Ideally, comparisons should also control for encoder architecture and training budget.

## 7. Main Scientific Hypothesis

**Can ordinal information constraints across nested neural subpopulations improve self-supervised representation learning beyond standard contrastive objectives, and does any resulting improvement depend on genuine inter-neuronal coordination?**

The proposed approach is motivated by the information-theoretic observation that adding observed neural variables cannot decrease the mutual information available about a separate reference population.

However, this theoretical property does not guarantee that an encoder trained with an ordinal loss will learn better representations.

The central contribution to investigate is therefore not simply the use of mutual information or variable-sized neuronal subsets, but whether an explicit information-ordering constraint helps preserve population-level structure that standard contrastive learning fails to capture.

## 8. Expected Contribution and Limitations

If successful, the method could provide a discriminative, reconstruction-free approach to learning neural population representations that emphasizes information shared across different neuronal subsets.

The most critical comparisons are:

**Experiment 2 vs. Experiment 3:** Does ranking improve representations beyond contrastive learning alone?

**Experiment 2 vs. Experiment 5:** Does the correct ordering matter?

**Experiment 2 vs. Experiment 4:** Are the learned representations sensitive to meaningful population coordination?

Potential confounds include simple firing-rate statistics, temporal autocorrelation, population-size cues, and changes in the difficulty of contrastive matching across subset sizes.

These factors must be controlled before attributing improvements specifically to inter-neuronal interactions.

**The proposal remains a research hypothesis. Its empirical effectiveness and novelty relative to existing neural representation learning methods require further validation.**
