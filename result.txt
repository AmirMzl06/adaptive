## Part 1 — Adaptive, dataset-specific ε and PGD for neural time series

### Question

Adversarial training in the image domain has been extensively investigated. For example, following the introduction of Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), several stronger and adaptive attack strategies have been proposed to evaluate and improve model robustness.

I would like to investigate a novel adversarial attack mechanism specifically for neural data. This domain presents several open research questions and opportunities. One important challenge is how to define an appropriate perturbation budget, (\epsilon). Unlike in the image domain, where perturbation magnitudes can often be defined in relatively standardized ways, neural datasets typically consist of multivariate time-series signals with dataset-specific statistical, temporal, and physiological characteristics. Therefore, choosing a fixed (\epsilon) across datasets may not be meaningful.

A promising direction would be to develop an adaptive, dataset-specific method for determining (\epsilon) and modifying PGD according to the characteristics of the neural signals. The perturbations should remain sufficiently small to preserve meaningful temporal, spectral, and physiological features of the original data, while still being strong enough to generate effective adversarial examples. Such adversarial examples could then be incorporated into adversarial training to improve the robustness of neural-data models.

I would therefore like to explore principled approaches for selecting (\epsilon) and adapting PGD to neural time-series data based on dataset characteristics, while ensuring that the resulting perturbations do not destroy semantically or physiologically meaningful information. Give me a novel approach.

### Response

Here's a framework I'd propose, followed by how it sits against what's already published. The short version: stop measuring ε in microvolts and measure it in units the dataset itself defines — how detectable the perturbation is against that dataset's own noise.

#### The core idea: ε as a detectability index (d′)

Replace the L∞/L2 ball with a ball in the metric induced by the dataset's estimated noise covariance Σ:

```
δᵀ Σ⁻¹ δ ≤ ε²
```

For Gaussian noise, √(δᵀΣ⁻¹δ) is exactly the ideal-observer (matched-filter) detectability d′ of a known perturbation δ. So ε becomes dimensionless and invariant to units, referencing, montage, sampling rate and subject — ε = 1 means the same thing on a motor-imagery dataset, a P300 dataset, MEG, or LFP: an unbiased ideal observer who knows the perturbation exactly would still only reach about 69% correct in a yes/no detection task. That gives you a defensible universal default (one just-noticeable difference) and a common axis on which attack-success curves from different datasets can finally be overlaid.

The metric also fixes PGD's direction, not just its budget. Steepest ascent under this norm is δ ∝ Σ ∇ₓL — the gradient *colored* by the noise covariance. The attack is pushed to put energy where the data already has it (low frequencies under the 1/f spectrum, dominant spatial modes under volume conduction) and is charged heavily for high-frequency or spatially implausible patterns. A flat µV budget does the opposite: a perturbation that's small relative to delta amplitude is huge relative to gamma amplitude, which matters because a recent spectral study on CHB-MIT found the 0–5 Hz range to be the dominant axis of adversarial vulnerability, with EEG models staying sensitive to delta-band perturbations even under strict spectral constraints.

#### Three layers, each handling a different kind of structure

**Layer 1 — dataset-level stationary metric.** Model Σ as Kronecker-structured, Σ = Σ_spatial ⊗ Σ_temporal. Σ_spatial is the C×C channel covariance with shrinkage (Ledoit–Wolf/OAS), estimated per subject or session on residuals (trial minus class mean for ERP data; whole trials for oscillatory tasks where background dominates). Σ_temporal is represented by a PSD from Welch or an AR(p) fit, so temporal whitening is just a prewhitening filter 1/√S(f). Whitening a trial costs O(C²T + CT log T), so you run ordinary PGD (with APGD-style step adaptation and restarts) on the whitened coordinates under an L2 ball of radius ε, and map back. Per-subject Σ means ε is automatically in subject-specific units, which absorbs inter-subject amplitude heterogeneity for free.

**Layer 2 — trial-level nonstationary masking.** A stationary Σ_temporal is wrong for bursty signals (seizures, sleep, ERD/ERS). Replace it per trial with a local time–frequency gain: compute the trial's multitaper spectrogram P_c(t,f), parametrize δ = ISTFT(m ⊙ z) with m_c(t,f) = √P_c(t,f), and run PGD on z with ‖z‖₂ ≤ ε. This is the neural analogue of psychoacoustic masking in audio attacks: local power masks local perturbation. Consequences: the attack cannot inject an alpha burst into a desynchronized epoch, cannot fill quiet periods, and the budget tracks nonstationarity. Conceptually this is still Layer 1 — it's d′ against a time-varying noise model equal to the trial's own local power — so the ε scale stays comparable.

**Layer 3 — physiological invariants that a quadratic form can't express.** Define a small set of guards whose tolerances come from *natural within-class, within-subject variability* rather than hand-picked thresholds:

- log band power per channel (change ≤ τ × within-class SD);
- aperiodic exponent and offset from specparam/FOOOF;
- the trial covariance on the SPD manifold: affine-invariant Riemannian distance d_R(C_x, C_{x+δ}) ≤ median same-class nearest-neighbour d_R. This one matters because Riemannian/CSP pipelines are the strongest classical BCI decoders, and a perturbation that moves the covariance less than a natural repeat can't be flagged by them;
- ERP peak latency/amplitude in the component window, for ERP tasks;
- an artifact-rejection pass (Autoreject thresholds, ICLabel) at the same rate as clean trials.

Enforce them with a backtracking rule inside PGD (if a guard is violated after a step, halve the step and retry) plus a final accept/reject; d_R is differentiable, so it can also be a soft penalty.

#### Choosing ε per dataset: a calibration protocol, not a number

Compute three independent criteria from clean data, all in d′ units:

1. **ε_JND = 1**, the ideal-observer threshold (sweep 0.5–3 for curves).
2. **ε_NN**: for each clean trial, the whitened distance to its nearest same-class, same-subject neighbour; take a low quantile (e.g., the 10th). The adversarial example is then closer to its original than the original is to a natural repeat — trial-to-trial variability made precise.
3. **ε_det**: the largest ε at which a detector trained to separate clean from adversarial trials (plus a clean-only anomaly detector such as an autoencoder) stays ≤ 0.55 AUC on held-out subjects. This is the adaptive-adversary criterion, and it stops "imperceptible" from meaning merely "invisible by eye".

Take ε* = min of the three. Then run a minimum-norm attack (FMN/DDN in the whitened metric) to measure each trial's distance to the decision boundary in d′. The ratio median(d′_boundary)/ε* is a dataset-intrinsic **robustness margin**: below 1 the decoder can be flipped within a physiologically indistinguishable budget; above 1, larger attacks aren't meaningful for that dataset. Reporting this margin across datasets and models is arguably the headline result — it's a quantity nobody currently has a principled way to compute.

#### Adversarial training

Drop d′-PGD into Madry-style AT or TRADES with a per-trial budget ε_i = min(ε*, ε_NN,i), i.e., instance-adaptive AT in the spirit of IAAT/MMA, with a curriculum from 0.25 up to ε*. Two things worth testing that fall out naturally:

- Because the threat model is "perturbations shaped like the dataset's own noise and nonstationarity", whitened AT should improve cross-session and cross-subject generalization more than L∞ AT does — session-to-session shift in EEG is largely a covariance shift. Train on session 1 of BCI-IV 2a, test on session 2; leave-one-subject-out.
- Sample Σ from the pool of per-subject covariances during training so robustness isn't tied to one subject's noise structure.

#### Sketch

```
Calibrate(dataset):
  Σ_s, S(f)  ← shrinkage channel cov, Welch PSD   (per subject/session)
  ε_NN       ← quantile_10( whitened NN distance, same class/subject )
  ε_det      ← max ε with detector AUC ≤ 0.55 (held-out subjects)
  ε*         ← min(1, ε_NN, ε_det)

dprimePGD(x, y, ε*):
  z ← 0
  for k in 1..K:
     δ   = Color(z)               # Σ_s^{1/2}, ×√S(f) or trial mask m
     g_z = ∇_z L(f(x+δ), y)       # autograd through Color
     z   = Proj_{‖z‖₂≤ε*}(z + α_k · g_z/‖g_z‖)
     if any Layer-3 guard violated: α_k ← α_k/2, retry (≤ K_bt)
  return x + Color(z) if all guards pass else reject
```

#### Validation plan

Datasets spanning regimes: BCI-IV 2a (oscillatory MI), BNCI P300 (ERP), SEED (long, emotion), CHB-MIT (nonstationary seizure), plus one MEG and one invasive set (ECoG/LFP) for the cross-modality claim. Models: EEGNet, ShallowConvNet, EEG-Conformer, a Riemannian MDM/tangent-space baseline, and a pretrained EEG foundation model. Baselines: L∞ PGD, low-pass/smooth PGD, wavelet TFAttack, Meng et al.'s physically constrained attack, TSA-STAT. Key figures: ASR vs ε(d′) overlaid across datasets on one axis; detectability AUC vs ε; the fidelity suite; transfer; the robustness-margin table; and cross-session AT gains.

#### Extensions and risks

For EEG/MEG with a leadfield L you can go further: δ = L·s + n (source-space perturbation plus sensor noise) with the budget in source space — physically realizable, not just plausible. For spike trains the same logic applies with a different metric: perturbations are jitter/insertion/deletion, budgeted by van Rossum distance with τ fitted to the dataset's inter-trial variability, calibrated the same three-way way.

Risks to address up front: EEG is heavy-tailed, so use robust covariance (Tyler's estimator) or Student-t whitening and treat the d′ reading as approximate; few-trial datasets need shrinkage and the Kronecker assumption should be checked against a full covariance on a small C·T slice; detectors overfit to one attack, so train them across attacks and report on held-out subjects; and evaluate with the min-norm attack alongside PGD to rule out gradient masking.

#### Where this sits relative to prior work

The pieces that exist: Meng et al. (NeurIPS 2022) were the first to model attacks on EEGNet under physical constraints, producing smooth, imperceptible perturbations and modelling propagation across the scalp; Yi et al. attack jointly in the time and wavelet domains to trade attack strength against imperceptibility; MS-UAP smooths universal perturbations by convolution to avoid physiologically implausible square-wave distortions; and for generic time series, TSA-STAT constrains statistical features of the series and gives certified bounds on them, while Pialla et al. showed standard attacks on time series produce visible sawtooth and spike artifacts and proposed smoother perturbations. All of these pick a budget in signal units or impose fixed smoothness. What I haven't found anywhere is (a) a budget expressed as a noise-calibrated detectability index that makes ε comparable across datasets, (b) a data-driven calibration of ε from natural trial-to-trial variability and detector AUC, (c) a Riemannian covariance guard, or (d) the resulting "robustness margin" as a dataset-level quantity. Those four are where the novelty is.

## Part 2 — Learnable, data-adaptive augmentation as an architectural component

### Question

Data augmentation is particularly useful in the neural-data domain because neural recordings are inherently noisy and often limited in size. Appropriate augmentation strategies can reduce overfitting and improve the generalization and robustness of neural decoding models. Existing approaches commonly use relatively simple transformations, such as additive Gaussian noise, temporal masking, channel dropout, or signal perturbations.

I would like to explore whether a more principled and learnable augmentation mechanism can be introduced as a novel architectural component. For example, instead of applying a predefined augmentation such as Gaussian noise, we could design a neural module—potentially based on Neural ODEs, stochastic differential equations (SDEs), diffusion processes, or learnable dynamical systems—that acts as either a denoiser, an augmentor, or both.

One possible direction is to develop a learnable ODE-based augmentation layer that models the evolution of neural signals along a continuous latent trajectory. During training, the model could generate controlled variations of the original neural representation by perturbing the initial state, the latent dynamics, or the integration trajectory. The augmentation strength could be conditioned on properties of the input signal, such as its variance, spectral content, signal-to-noise ratio, or recording session. This would allow the model to generate realistic and sample-specific perturbations rather than applying the same noise distribution to every example.

Another direction would be to design a denoising–augmentation dual module. The module could learn to separate task-relevant neural structure from nuisance variability. One branch would reconstruct a cleaner representation, while another would generate plausible variations within the learned nuisance subspace. The augmented samples could then be used during training to encourage the downstream decoder to remain invariant to session-specific noise and other irrelevant variations while preserving information relevant to the decoding task.

The key research question would therefore be whether augmentation can be moved from a manually designed preprocessing operation to a learnable, data-adaptive component of the neural decoding architecture. A particularly interesting contribution would be to constrain the learned transformations so that they preserve meaningful temporal, spectral, and neural features while generating sufficiently diverse samples to improve model generalization across trials, sessions, and subjects.

### Response

Here's a framework I'd propose. The one-line version: stop treating augmentation as a transformation applied *to* the data, and define it as **sampling from the posterior over nuisance variability in a learned latent dynamical model of the dataset**. Denoising is then the posterior mean, augmentation is a posterior sample, and the augmentation strength is sample-specific by construction rather than by design.

#### 1. The core principle: augmentation = nuisance posterior sampling

Fit a latent dynamical model whose latent state is factorized into a **content** path c(t), which must carry the decoding target, and a **nuisance** path n(t), which must carry everything else (session, subject, electrode drift, background oscillations, measurement noise). Given a trial x, the model defines a posterior over both. An augmentation of x is a new trial decoded from the *same* content path and a *resampled* nuisance path:

```
x̃ = Dec( c(t) | x ,  ñ(t) ),   ñ ~ p_τ( n | x, s′ )
```

Three things fall out of this definition that hand-designed augmentations can't give you:

- **Strength is trial-conditioned for free.** The posterior over n is wide for low-SNR trials and tight for clean ones, so with a single global temperature τ the realized perturbation scales with each trial's own uncertainty. You get "noise proportional to SNR" without ever specifying it.
- **Content is preserved structurally, not by hoping.** c(t) is held fixed, so task-relevant temporal structure (ERP timing, ERD/ERS evolution, reach dynamics) is untouched. Preservation is a property of the operator, not a loss term you tune.
- **The same module is a denoiser.** τ = 0 (posterior-mean nuisance) returns a cleaned trial; τ = 1 returns a natural sibling of the trial; τ > 1 or sampling from the prior returns wider variations; and swapping the session code s → s′ transplants the trial into another session's nuisance statistics, which is cross-session style transfer inside the model.

#### 2. Architecture: a content–nuisance latent SDE

A concrete instantiation along the ODE/SDE lines you're interested in:

- **Content (deterministic ODE):** dc = f_θ(c) dt, with c(0) ~ q(c₀ | x). Deterministic so that any stochasticity in the output is attributable to nuisance.
- **Nuisance (session-conditioned SDE):** dn = −A_s n dt + G_s(n, φ(x)) dW_t. An Ornstein–Uhlenbeck-like process whose drift A_s and diffusion G_s are session/subject-conditioned, and whose diffusion is additionally conditioned on trial descriptors φ(x) (variance, spectral slope, estimated noise floor). This is where "augmentation strength conditioned on signal properties" lives, and it is learned.
- **Observation:** x_t = W_s · h(c_t, n_t) + e_t. A session-specific mixing matrix W_s (parameterized as W(I + E_s) with small E_s) captures electrode placement and referencing differences, which are the dominant cross-session nuisance in EEG and are pure channel mixing, not additive noise. For spikes, replace with a Poisson observation on exp(h(·)) and add a per-unit slow gain-drift process and unit dropout to the nuisance block, which is what day-to-day instability actually looks like.
- **Inference:** an encoder gives q(c₀ | x) and a posterior drift for the nuisance SDE; the KL between posterior and prior path measures is the standard Girsanov term from latent-SDE training, so the whole thing trains as an ELBO.

Augmentation operator, with its three knobs: (i) temperature τ on the nuisance diffusion; (ii) session swap s → s′; (iii) partial observation — mask a random fraction of channels or time before encoding, so the posterior widens where the model has to impute. Backprop goes through the reparameterized Brownian increments, so the layer is differentiable like dropout.

#### 3. The objective, and why it won't collapse to identity

This is the part where most "learnable augmentation" ideas die: if the augmentor is trained to minimize the decoder's loss, it learns to do nothing. Train the three parts on different objectives:

- The **generative model** on the ELBO across *all* sessions and subjects, labels optional, so the large unlabeled pool is used.
- The **decoder** on the task loss over augmented samples.
- The **augmentation knobs** (τ, the nuisance diffusion scale, swap probability) on a *maximum-invariance* objective: make the nuisance distribution as wide as possible subject to the decoder's loss on augmented samples not exceeding its clean loss by more than a margin, with the width entering as an entropy bonus. This is the Augerino principle, but applied to a learned nuisance manifold instead of a hand-chosen transformation family. Tune the margin by bilevel optimization on a held-out *session*, not held-out trials, since the generalization target is sessions.

Two kinds of constraint keep the learned variations physiological:

- **Leakage audits as training signals.** A probe predicting y from n must sit at chance (gradient-reversal head or an MI penalty), and a probe predicting session s from c must sit at chance. Without the first, the model hides content in the nuisance block and "augmentation" changes labels; without the second, content carries session identity and the invariance goal is defeated. Report both probes in the paper as diagnostics, since they're the actual evidence the factorization worked.
- **A physiological trust region.** Bound the augmented trial to the whitened, noise-covariance ball from the attack framework (d′ ≤ ε_NN, the natural same-class nearest-neighbour distance), and keep the same feature guards (log band powers, aperiodic exponent, Riemannian covariance distance, ERP latency) within the within-class spread. Sharing the budget definition between the attack and the augmentation is also a nice unifying story: both are "perturbations indistinguishable from natural variability", one adversarial, one sampled.

And an optional hardening step that connects directly to the adversarial-training thread: run the d′-PGD attack *restricted to the nuisance latent* for the hardest trials. That is adversarial training where the adversary can only move along directions the model has learned are nuisance, which is both more realistic and less likely to generate label-flipping perturbations than raw-signal adversarial training.

#### 4. Three instantiations, in increasing order of commitment

**Lite (build first): noise-floor SDEdit.** Train a session-conditioned score model on whitened trials. Augment by adding Gaussian noise up to a level matched to the trial's own estimated noise floor (σ² = κ·σ̂²_noise(x)), then run the reverse diffusion back to t = 0; conditioning the reverse process on s′ gives the session swap. Learn κ by the bilevel criterion. No explicit factorization, but you get sample-conditioned strength and a denoiser (full reverse from small t) from one network, and it's a few hundred lines on top of any diffusion codebase.

**Middle: Kalman-sampler augmentation.** Make the nuisance block linear-Gaussian with a nonlinear decoder. The posterior is then exact (Kalman smoother → denoised mean; forward-filter–backward-sample → augmentation), there is no SDE solver or adjoint, and the content block can be a simple RNN or ODE. Cheap enough to run inside every training step.

**Full: the content–nuisance latent SDE** of Section 2, when you want the explicit factorization, session-conditioned diffusion, and the leakage audits as first-class objects.

A bonus at test time: run the layer deterministically for denoising, or Monte-Carlo it for free test-time augmentation and a calibrated uncertainty estimate. For BCI, "how uncertain is this trial" is nearly as valuable as the label.

#### 5. How I'd evaluate it

Settings that isolate the claim: cross-session (BCI-IV 2a session 1 → 2), cross-subject (MASS or Sleep-EDF sleep staging, which gives a direct comparison to the differentiable-augmentation literature; SEED), low-data learning curves, and for spikes a multi-day motor dataset for cross-day decoding plus NLB benchmarks. Baselines: the standard fixed-augmentation suite (noise, time/frequency shift, channel dropout, masking, FT-surrogate, mixup), CADDA and AugNet as learned-policy baselines, DiffEEG-style unconditional generation, Euclidean/Riemannian alignment, LFADS/NDT pretraining for spikes. Metrics beyond accuracy: the two leakage probes, a representation-invariance score under nuisance resampling, calibration with MC augmentation, and the fidelity suite on the augmented samples. Ablations that carry the argument: resample nuisance only vs. all latents; posterior vs. prior sampling; session swap on/off; trust region on/off; trial-conditioned vs. global diffusion scale.

#### 6. Risks

Posterior collapse of the nuisance block (use free bits or KL annealing); content hidden in nuisance (the probes catch it, but only if you run them on held-out sessions); the generative model overfitting training sessions (hold out sessions in stage 1 too); latent-SDE adjoints being slow (that's why the middle instantiation exists); and the perennial one, evaluation leakage across sessions, which makes every cross-session result look better than it is.

#### 7. Where this sits relative to prior work

The two closest lines each cover half. On the augmentation side, Rommel et al. learned differentiable, class-wise augmentation policies for EEG where good policies are largely unknown, and then built AugNet, an end-to-end differentiable augmentation layer evaluated on cross-subject sleep staging; diffusion-based work like DiffEEG generates diverse synthetic EEG to supplement classifiers. All of these either search over hand-designed transformation families or generate unconditionally. On the dynamics side, LFADS infers single-trial latent dynamics from spikes with a sequential VAE and stitches across sessions spanning months, TNDM decomposes neural dynamics into behaviourally relevant and irrelevant components, SNP-GPFA separates signal and noise latent structure, and Jude et al. perturb spike data in ways mirroring session-to-session variability to compensate for it. None of these use the factorized model *as the augmentation operator*, condition the nuisance diffusion on the trial, train the augmentation width on a maximum-invariance objective, or audit content/nuisance leakage as part of the method. That combination is the contribution.
